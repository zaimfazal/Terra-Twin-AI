# General background, not candidate evidence

This note is general planetary background. It does not describe any measured value at an Earth candidate. Candidate-specific claims must come from the evidence pack.

## Moon

- The Moon has essentially no atmosphere; its exosphere is extremely tenuous. There is no appreciable aerodynamic drag, weather or aeolian sediment transport.
- Surface gravity is about one sixth of Earth's (approximately 1.62 m/s²).
- Without a substantial atmosphere, the surface is exposed to vacuum, micrometeoroids and solar and cosmic radiation.
- Surface temperatures vary strongly between illuminated and shadowed terrain. Permanently shadowed regions near the poles are extremely cold.
- Illumination geometry, long shadows and permanent shadow are first-order operational constraints at the lunar south pole.
- The Moon has no liquid-water oceans or atmosphere-driven water cycle. Water ice is investigated through indirect remote sensing and, where available, surface measurements.

## Mars

- Mars has a thin carbon-dioxide-dominated atmosphere, far less dense than Earth's. Surface pressure is a small fraction of Earth's.
- Surface gravity is about 38 percent of Earth's (approximately 3.71 m/s²).
- The thin atmosphere provides much less radiation shielding than Earth's atmosphere and magnetic field.
- Diurnal and seasonal temperature ranges are large, and dust storms and dust deposition affect operations.
- Aeolian processes are active; wind-formed bedforms and dust cover are relevant surface processes.
- Liquid water is not stable at the surface under present conditions; water ice and hydrated materials are important investigation targets.

## Earth

- Earth has a dense nitrogen-oxygen atmosphere, a global magnetic field, abundant liquid water and active plate tectonics and weathering.
- Earth surface gravity is about 9.81 m/s².
- Terrestrial landscapes are shaped by atmosphere, biosphere, liquid water and long-term tectonic and climatic processes that have no direct counterpart on the Moon and only partial counterparts on Mars.
- An Earth site can be a useful process or terrain analog without reproducing the planetary environment.

# Consequence for the assistant

Similarity scores compare what the scientific engine measured under its planetary-aware method. They do not imply that an Earth site reproduces lunar or Martian gravity, vacuum, radiation, atmospheric pressure, thermal environment or aeolian processes. Every analogy explanation should state the relevant differences and should never call an Earth site a perfect replica.
