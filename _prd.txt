[Heading 1] 1. Executive Summary
[Heading 2] 1.1 Product vision
[Normal] Build an intelligent, scientifically grounded platform that systematically searches Earth's land surface for environments analogous to the Moon and Mars.
[Normal] The platform will use open Earth-observation and planetary datasets from NASA and other space agencies to identify, rank, visualize, compare, and characterize promising terrestrial analog environments.
[Normal] It will combine:
[List Bullet] Global terrestrial analog discovery.
[List Bullet] Interactive Earth-Moon-Mars exploration.
[List Bullet] Scientific similarity calculations.
[List Bullet] Mission-specific analysis.
[List Bullet] Evidence-based discovery reports.
[List Bullet] AI-assisted scientific interpretation.
[Normal] The platform should go beyond finding locations that visually resemble other planets. It should explain why an environment is analogous, quantify the strength of that analogy, identify missing evidence, and explain where the comparison breaks down.
[Heading 2] 1.2 Core product proposition
[Normal] Discover where Earth can help us understand and prepare for exploring the Moon and Mars.
[Heading 2] 1.3 Primary differentiator
[Normal] Rather than merely displaying known planetary analog locations, the platform will systematically search Earth's land surface and identify potentially overlooked environments.
[Normal] Every discovery should be supported by measurable scientific evidence, transparent calculations, and traceable datasets.
[Heading 1] 2. Problem Statement
[Normal] Space agencies already use terrestrial environments such as Atacama, Antarctica, Death Valley, Iceland, and Haughton Crater to test exploration technologies and study planetary processes.
[Normal] However, these environments do not reproduce all the conditions found on the Moon or Mars. Different locations may offer useful analogies for different geological, environmental, or operational characteristics.
[Normal] Existing approaches are often focused on particular well-known locations or specialized research objectives.
[Normal] There is an opportunity to create a systematic discovery platform that combines:
[Normal] 1. Global Earth observations.
[Normal] 2. Lunar and Martian scientific observations.
[Normal] 3. Geospatial and statistical analysis.
[Normal] 4. Mission-specific similarity calculations.
[Normal] 5. Reproducible scientific reporting.
[Normal] The challenge is to discover meaningful analogies while being honest about the limitations of terrestrial environments.

[Heading 1] 8. Scientific Similarity Framework
[Normal] This is the core scientific subsystem.
[Heading 2] 8.1 Scientific dimensions
[Normal] The MVP will implement five primary dimensions.
[Normal] Solar illumination is especially important for lunar south pole operations. It may also be evaluated for Mars where the selected target and mission objective make it relevant.
[Normal] Each feature will be used only when an appropriate dataset and scientifically defensible comparison method exist. Unavailable measurements must not be fabricated.
[Heading 2] 8.2 Planetary-aware comparison
[Normal] The engine must account for the fundamental differences between Earth, Moon, and Mars.
[Normal] Examples:
[List Bullet] Atmospheric differences.
[List Bullet] Gravity differences.
[List Bullet] Thermal behavior.
[List Bullet] Solar exposure.
[List Bullet] Wind and dust processes.
[List Bullet] Differences in water and ice environments.
[Normal] The engine will use direct numerical comparison where valid and process-based or proxy-based comparison where direct equivalence is inappropriate.
[Normal] An Earth environment must never be described as a perfect planetary replica.
[Heading 2] 8.3 Similarity calculation
[Normal] The selected approach is a hybrid scientific similarity model.
[Normal] Processing steps:
[Normal] 1. Extract measurable features from Earth and planetary datasets.
[Normal] 2. Convert measurements into comparable representations.
[Normal] 3. Apply scientifically appropriate normalization.
[Normal] 4. Calculate feature-specific similarity.
[Normal] 5. Aggregate features into dimension scores.
[Normal] 6. Apply mission-specific scientific weights.
[Normal] 7. Calculate an overall similarity score.
[Normal] 8. Report evidence confidence and data coverage separately.
[Normal] Conceptual formula:
[Normal] S_overall = sum(w_i * S_i) / sum(w_i)
[Normal] Where:
[List Bullet] S_i is the similarity score for a dimension.
[List Bullet] w_i is its mission-specific weight.
[List Bullet] The sum includes only dimensions with valid measurements.
[Normal] This formula applies only after the required evidence eligibility checks have passed. Missing dimensions will not silently receive zero values.
[Heading 2] 8.4 Three independent result metrics
[Normal] Every candidate must report:
[Normal] Similarity score
[Normal] How closely the measured environment resembles the selected planetary target.
[Normal] Data coverage
[Normal] How much of the intended scientific feature set is available and usable.
[Normal] Data confidence
[Normal] How reliable the comparison is, taking account of source quality, resolution, uncertainty, temporal relevance, and evidence type.
[Normal] These must never be collapsed into a misleading single score.
[Heading 2] 8.5 Feature weighting
[Normal] Weights will vary across the four mission profiles. Users will be able to adjust weights in advanced analysis.
[Normal] The original default weights must always be visible and recorded in the analysis provenance.
[Normal] The team must document scientific justification for weights and test their effects using known analog locations.
[Heading 2] 8.6 Multi-scale similarity
[Normal] Comparison must support three spatial scales.
[Normal] The engine will report scale-specific scores.
[Heading 2] 8.7 Temporal comparison
[Normal] The platform will support multi-temporal analysis.
[Normal] Global screening:
[List Bullet] Multi-year averages.
[List Bullet] Seasonal statistics.
[List Bullet] Long-term patterns.
[Normal] Detailed investigation:
[List Bullet] Time-series observations.
[List Bullet] Environmental extremes.
[List Bullet] Seasonal variation.
[List Bullet] Relevant daily variations where data permits.
[Normal] Calendar dates will not be treated as equivalent across Earth, Moon, and Mars.
[Heading 1] 9. Global Earth Discovery Engine
[Heading 2] 9.1 Discovery architecture
[Normal] The engine will use coarse-to-fine global discovery.
[Heading 3] Stage 1: Global screening
[List Bullet] Scan Earth's land surface.
[List Bullet] Use approximately 5-10 km analysis resolution.
[List Bullet] Apply globally available environmental and geological datasets.
[List Bullet] Generate initial scientific feature indices.
[Heading 3] Stage 2: Candidate identification
[List Bullet] Target approximately 1 km analysis resolution.
[List Bullet] Identify promising grid cells.
[List Bullet] Identify spatially neighboring high-potential cells.
[List Bullet] Cluster them into geographical regions.
[Heading 3] Stage 3: Detailed investigation
[List Bullet] Target approximately 100-500 m where reliable data supports it.
[List Bullet] Retrieve additional observations.
[List Bullet] Analyze individual candidate regions.
[List Bullet] Calculate multi-scale scientific comparisons.
[Normal] The stated resolutions are analysis targets, not guarantees of native data resolution. Original dataset resolution must always be retained in metadata.
[Heading 2] 9.2 Hybrid region identification
[Normal] The engine will:
[Normal] 1. Create global grid-based screening units.
[Normal] 2. Calculate similarity for eligible cells.
[Normal] 3. Identify neighboring promising cells.
[Normal] 4. Cluster them into contiguous regions.
[Normal] 5. Refine boundaries using geological and terrain information.
[Normal] 6. Identify representative points within the region.
[Normal] This produces geographical discoveries rather than a random collection of map pins.

[Heading 2] 10.4 Novelty classification
[Normal] Three discovery categories are required.
[Normal] Established analogs
[Normal] Known terrestrial environments used as model validation benchmarks. Examples include Atacama Desert, Antarctica, Haughton Crater, Iceland, and Death Valley.
[Normal] Underexplored analogs
[Normal] Locations with promising scientific similarities but limited existing analog research.
[Normal] Potential new discoveries
[Normal] Locations identified by the engine that are not represented in the platform's current analog-site database.
[Normal] The system must not claim absolute scientific novelty simply because a location is absent from its database.

[List Bullet] Copernicus DEM is a digital surface model, not a pure bare-earth terrain model.

[List Bullet] MODIS land surface temperature is not equivalent to air temperature.
[List Bullet] Glacier inventories and permafrost maps are not interchangeable indicators of subsurface water or ice.

[Heading 1] 33. Final Product Definition
[Normal] The Earth-Moon-Mars Analog Discovery Platform is a public-facing, scientifically grounded web application that searches Earth's land surface for terrestrial environments analogous to lunar and Martian exploration targets.
[Normal] It combines global geospatial discovery, planetary reference comparisons, explainable scientific similarity calculations, data-confidence assessment, mission-specific rankings, independent evidence review, and AI-assisted scientific interpretation.
[Normal] Its central purpose is to identify potentially overlooked Earth environments that can help scientists and engineers prepare for future lunar bases and Martian exploration.
[Normal] The platform must demonstrate not only where Earth resembles the Moon or Mars, but also:
[List Bullet] Why the similarity exists.
[List Bullet] How strong the analogy is.
[List Bullet] What scientific evidence supports it.
[List Bullet] What evidence is missing.
[List Bullet] Where the analogy breaks down.
[List Bullet] How the location could contribute to real exploration research.
[Normal] Our ultimate objective is to turn Earth into a searchable laboratory for planetary exploration.
